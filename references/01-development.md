# 01 — Development: Concept to Outline

Where a premise becomes something that can be drafted. Output of this module is never a script — it is the material a script can be written from.

---

## 1. The logline test

Before structure, before characters, before genre: can the idea survive one sentence?

**A working logline has four parts:**

```text
[PROTAGONIST with a specific flaw or want] must [ACTIVE GOAL] before [CONSEQUENCE OF FAILURE],
but [OBSTACLE that is personal, not incidental].
```

Examples that pass:

> A debt-ridden delivery courier must hand a package to a stranger before midnight, but the recipient turns out to be the ex who left him three years ago — and she doesn't know he's the one who's been paying her rent.

> A werewolf pack enforcer must escort the alpha's human bride to the ceremony before the full moon, but she's the one person whose blood can break the bond she's being sold into.

Examples that fail, and why:

| Logline | Failure |
|---|---|
| "A young woman discovers she has powers." | No goal, no consequence, no obstacle. This is a premise, not a story. |
| "A man must save the world from AI." | No personal obstacle. Nothing about *him* is at stake. |
| "Two people fall in love over the summer." | No active goal. Falling is not doing. |

If the logline fails, do not proceed to outline. Fix the logline. Ten minutes here saves three drafts later.

## 2. Premise to structure

### 2.1 Find the grain spine

List the event grains (see `00-scale-control.md` §3) in order. This is the story's actual skeleton, independent of scenes.

```text
G1: she opens the door / he recognizes her            (reveal)
G2: she recognizes him, and pretends she doesn't      (relationship shift)
G3: he has to choose: hand it over or keep it         (decision with cost)
G4: she opens the box and finds her own returned letters (reveal, irreversible)
```

Four grains, 80 s ceiling. The spine tells you immediately whether the piece is a 60-second episode or a 10-minute short — before you write a word.

**Rule:** if a proposed beat contains no grain, it is texture. Keep it only if it buys something the grains can't (a breath, a laugh, a visual).

### 2.2 Choose the structure that fits the runtime

| Runtime | Structure | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 15–30 s | Single beat | One grain, entered late, exited early |
| 30–60 s | Hook + reversal | Grain 1 inside 2 s, grain 2 as the turn, ending on a question |
| 60–120 s | Hook + escalation + cliff | 3–4 grains, last one unresolved |
| 2–5 min | Three-beat | Setup / complication / consequence, each a grain cluster |
| 5–15 min | Five-beat | Plus a midpoint reversal and a low point |
| 10–30 min | Full arc | Eight-sequence or three-act, with a deliberate subplot |
| Series | Grain-per-episode + season spine | Each episode moves one grain; the season resolves one question |

Do not use three-act structure for a 60-second piece. It will feel like a trailer for a film that doesn't exist.

### 2.3 Enter late, leave early

Every scene starts after the greeting and ends before the goodbye. The most common development error in short-form is starting at the beginning of the interaction instead of at the point of pressure.

Test each scene with: *what is the first line or image that could not be cut?* Start there.

## 3. Character work that actually pays off

For short-form, full character biographies are waste. You need four things per character, and they must be operational — each one has to change a line of dialogue or a shot choice.

### 3.1 The four-field character card

```text
@character: <NAME>
Wants (external, in this story):
Needs (internal, unknowable to them):
Lie they believe:              <the false thing that makes them act>
Tells:                         <one physical or verbal habit the camera can catch>
Voice:                         <3 adjectives, plus one thing they never say>
```

**"Lie they believe"** is the load-bearing field. It is why they make the wrong decision at the midpoint. If you can't fill it, the character is a function, not a person — which is acceptable for a henchman and fatal for a lead.

**"Tells"** is what makes them filmable. "Nervous" is not filmable. "Rubs her thumb across her knuckles when lying" is.

### 3.2 Relationship is the engine, not the character

Short-form drama lives or dies on a two-person dynamic. Define it as a single line:

```text
@relationship: <A> and <B> — <what each needs from the other> / <what each refuses to give>
```

> Courier and ex — he needs her to admit she waited; she needs him to leave without asking why she didn't.

That line generates twenty scenes. A character biography generates none.

### 3.3 Cast discipline

| Tier | Max speaking characters | Max on-screen named |
|---|---|---|
| S | 3 | 4 |
| M | 6 | 8 |
| L | 10 | 14 |

Beyond these, the audience cannot hold the names and the budget cannot hold the casting. Every extra character is a costume, a continuity risk, and a shot-list complication.

## 4. Genre and trope map

Short-form drama runs on tropes. This is not a failure of imagination — it is how the format signals its promise in two seconds. The craft is in the *specific execution* of a known trope, not in avoiding it.

### 4.1 Vertical drama tropes (English-language market)

| Trope | Engine | What must be specific |
|---|---|---|
| Secret billionaire / hidden CEO | Status reveal | The *reason* for hiding it must be personal, not convenient |
| Contract marriage | Proximity + obligation | The clause that will be broken |
| Revenge return | Delayed justice | What the revenge costs the avenger |
| Second-chance romance | Unfinished business | The precise thing that was never said |
| Werewolf / fated mate | Involuntary bond | The choice that resists the bond |
| Mafia / forced protection | Coercion + safety | The moment protection becomes possession |
| Secret child | Delayed knowledge | The child's own want, not just the parent's |
| Reborn / second life | Foreknowledge | What they choose *not* to change |

The trope supplies the promise. The specific detail supplies the reason to watch episode 2.

### 4.2 Trope failure modes

- **The trope does the work.** If the story only functions because "he's a billionaire," the story is a label.
- **Two tropes, no engine.** Contract marriage *and* amnesia *and* secret child is not a premise, it's a pitch deck.
- **The reveal is the plot.** A status reveal is a hinge, not a story. What happens *after* the reveal is the story.

### 4.3 Genre pacing notes

| Genre | Grain cadence | Dialogue share | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Romance drama | 1 grain / 20–25 s | 60–70% | Silence only for the beat before a confession |
| Thriller | 1 grain / 15–20 s | 35–50% | Information is the currency; every scene must trade it |
| Revenge | 1 grain / 20 s | 50–60% | Escalation must be measurable; each act costs more |
| Comedy | 1 grain / 12–18 s | 55–70% | The grain is the joke landing; don't count the setup |
| Horror | 1 grain / 25–40 s | 20–35% | Grain cadence is slow; sound does the work |
| Family / drama | 1 grain / 30 s | 55–65% | Reaction shots carry as much as lines |

## 5. Platform pace profile

Every project gets one. It is not marketing copy — it is the constraint set the drafting module will obey.

```text
[PACE PROFILE]
Platform:          <TikTok / Reels / Shorts / ReelShort / DramaBox / YouTube / ad network>
Runtime per unit:  X s
Hook window:       0:00–0:0X
Shot density:      X.X s/shot  (and any per-section variation)
Dialogue share:    X%
Subtitle strategy: burned-in / platform auto / none
Loop-ability:      yes / no / n-a
End behavior:      resolve / cliff / CTA
Audio assumption:  sound-on / sound-off first watch
```

Two examples:

```text
[PACE PROFILE]
Platform: ReelShort, episode 1
Runtime per unit: 75 s
Hook window: 0:00–0:02 (she is already bleeding)
Shot density: 2.5 s/shot for 0–15 s, then 3.5
Dialogue share: 65%
Subtitle strategy: burned-in, high-contrast
Loop-ability: no
End behavior: cliff — the door opens on her sister
Audio assumption: sound-on, but text carries premise
```

```text
[PACE PROFILE]
Platform: TikTok, brand narrative
Runtime per unit: 32 s
Hook window: 0:00–0:01.5 (the glass breaks before we see whose hand)
Shot density: 2.5 throughout
Dialogue share: 25%
Subtitle strategy: burned-in captions, product name locked at 0:26
Loop-ability: yes — last frame matches first
End behavior: CTA card, 3 s
Audio assumption: sound-off first watch
```

## 6. Output: the project bible

Deliver this at the end of development. It is the contract the rest of the pipeline works against.

```text
[PROJECT BIBLE]
Title:
Tier / Runtime / G count:
Logline:
Premise (3 sentences max):
Pace profile:                (block above)
Structure:                   (named, with grain spine)
Characters:                  (cards, lead first)
Relationships:               (one line each, leads only)
Locations:                   (count, and whether each is recycled)
Hero props:                  (count, and which grains depend on them)
Tone references:             (2–3 existing works, and what specifically to borrow)
Music direction:             (one line; genre + energy, not a track list)
Open decisions:              (what the user still has to choose)
```

**Open decisions should be short and real.** If there are more than five, development isn't finished — you're pushing your work onto the user as a questionnaire.

## 7. Development anti-patterns

| Pattern | What it looks like | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Premise inflation | Adding tropes to make a weak idea feel bigger | Every trope is a promise the script must pay |
| Biography dump | 800 words on a character's childhood | None of it changes a line |
| Outline as prose | 2,000-word "treatment" with no scene breaks | Can't be budgeted, can't be drafted |
| Structure worship | Insisting on three acts for a 45-second piece | The piece becomes a synopsis of itself |
| Theme statement | "This story is about the nature of forgiveness" | Not actionable; delete or convert to a behavior |
| Location sprawl | Six locations for a 90-second episode | Budget and continuity death |
| Unnamed characters | "MAN 1", "WOMAN 2" | They will merge into each other on screen |

```text
[HANDOFF]
Stage: Development complete
Pace profile: (as locked)
Locked so far: logline, grain spine, character cards, pace profile
Main risks: <e.g. two leads both want the same thing — no friction; location 3 is expensive>
Next step:
- Suggested module: 02-script-drafting
- Materials needed: confirmation on <open decision>, runtime confirmation
- Copy-paste invocation: "Using the project bible, draft the full script at <runtime> s, Tier <X>, obeying the pace profile."
```
